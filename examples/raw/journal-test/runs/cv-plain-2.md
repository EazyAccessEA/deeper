## Answer

# CV Feedback: Senior Marketing Executive, Hartwell Outdoor

**Assumptions:** I've assumed anything not on your CV either didn't happen or needs evidence. Square brackets like [£X] mark figures only you can supply. Please don't fill them with estimates you can't support at interview.

## The big picture

You have most of what they're asking for, but the CV understates it. Your Klaviyo bullet is strong. The other essentials are vague or missing, and those are the ones a recruiter screening against a checklist will look for. The biggest risk is paid social budget, which is probably the main reason this role is "Senior."

## Essential criteria, one by one

**1. 3+ years in B2C marketing: ✅ Met**
- You have about 3 years 9 months at Fernbrook and 2 years 6 months at Lumen, so roughly 6 years in total.
- **Gap:** Nothing covers Mar 2022 to Feb 2023, about 11 months. Add a one-line explanation (travel, caring, freelance, study, job hunting). Otherwise the reader will wonder.

**2. Paid social budgets of £20k+/month on Meta and TikTok: ⚠️ Biggest weakness**
- "Ran paid social campaigns on Meta and TikTok" gives no budget, no planning and no results. The advert names a specific figure, so the screener will look for a number.
- Add your monthly budget, whether you **planned and allocated** it (not just ran ads), and results such as ROAS, CPA, revenue or follower growth.
- **If your budget was under £20k**, still state it. Show growth and efficiency (for example, "scaled from £[X]k to £[Y]k/month while cutting CPA by [Z]%"). Then make the case in your cover letter. A small budget with good numbers beats a vague line.
- Example: *"Planned and managed £[X]k/month paid social budget across Meta and TikTok; [result, e.g. ROAS of X / £Y revenue / CPA down Z%]."*

**3. GA4 reporting: ⚠️ Weak and possibly outdated**
- Your only analytics bullet is from Lumen, which ended in March 2022. GA4 didn't fully replace Universal Analytics until July 2023, so that work was probably in the old version. A sharp reader will notice.
- "Pulled weekly reports… for the marketing manager" sounds like data retrieval, not analysis.
- If you use GA4 at Fernbrook, add a bullet there. Say what you reported on and what decisions came from it. Example: *"Built GA4 reports on campaign and channel performance; insights led to [budget shift / landing page change / result]."*
- In Skills, write **"GA4"** explicitly instead of "Google Analytics."

**4. Writing briefs for designers and agencies: ✅ Met, could be stronger**
- Good evidence. Add volume or outcome, such as how many campaigns or what the packaging achieved.
- The advert mentions **agencies**. If you've briefed any agency (printer, PR, ads), say so.

**5. Email campaigns in Klaviyo: ✅ Strongest point**
- The subject-line result is exactly the sort of evidence that works.
- One caution: open rates have been inflated since Apple's Mail Privacy Protection launched in 2021, and experienced email marketers know this. If you have click rate, conversion or email revenue figures, add one of them.
- If you've built flows (welcome, abandoned basket, post-purchase), mention them. They matter a lot for an online retailer.

## Desirable criteria

- **Retail experience:** You have it, but you haven't made the most of it. Fernbrook (online plus 3 cafes) closely mirrors Hartwell (online plus 14 shops). Add anything you did for the physical locations, such as in-store promotions or driving online customers into the cafes.
- **Outdoor brands:** Nothing on the CV. If you have a genuine interest (hiking, climbing, camping, running), mention it briefly in your profile or cover letter. It helps with a lifestyle brand.
- **Managing 1–2 people:** Your only evidence is a summer intern in 2021. Add any current people responsibilities, such as onboarding, supervising freelancers or mentoring. Managing your freelance designer arguably counts.
