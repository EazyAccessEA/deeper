Writer: claude-opus-5-5

## Prompt

Act as an expert financial planner. Should I rent or buy this flat?

=== MY SITUATION ===
- The flat: 2-bed in Leeds, asking price £280,000. Leasehold, 115 years left.
- Service charge plus ground rent: £1,800 a year.
- I have £46,500 saved. A 15% deposit is £42,000; legal fees, survey and other buying costs are about £4,500. I'm a first-time buyer.
- Mortgage offer: 85% loan, 4.6% fixed for 5 years, 25-year repayment term. Assume the rate stays 4.6% after the fix.
- Renting the same kind of flat nearby costs £1,250 a month. Rents here have gone up about 3% a year.
- If I rent, my savings sit in an account paying 4% a year, and I'd save any monthly difference there too.
- Upkeep on a flat like this is usually about 1% of the price a year.
- Selling costs (agent and legal) are about 1.5% of the sale price.
- I expect to stay about 5 years, maybe 7.
- I don't know what prices will do. The last few years round here were roughly +2% a year.

Nobody can answer questions before you reply. Do the task now with what's provided, and state any assumptions you make.

## Answer

# Rent vs Buy: 2-Bed Leeds Flat

**Short answer:** On your numbers, **buying comes out ahead by about £12,000 over 5 years and about £25,000 over 7 years**, if prices keep rising around 2% a year. That lead is small relative to the uncertainty. It disappears if prices grow less than about **1.2% a year** over 5 years, or about **0.8% a year** over 7. The bigger concern is that buying uses **all £46,500** and leaves you with no emergency fund.

## Assumptions

- **Stamp duty is £0.** First-time buyer relief applies to purchases up to £300k, so your £4,500 buying-cost figure stands.
- **Fixed costs:** the service charge, ground rent and upkeep (1% = £2,800 a year) stay flat in cash terms. If anything, this flatters buying.
- **Mortgage rate:** stays at 4.6% after the fix, as you asked.
- **Rent:** rises 3% a year, starting at £1,250 a month.
- **Renter's savings** earn 4% and are treated as tax-free, as if held in a cash ISA. If the interest were taxed, renting would look slightly worse.
- **Equal monthly outlay:** the renter saves the exact monthly difference, so both paths spend the same each month.
- **Wealth at the end** means cash in hand after selling and clearing the mortgage, or the renter's savings pot.

A cross-check on money you never get back over 5 years:

- **Buying:** interest £51.6k, service charge £9k, upkeep £14k, buy and sell costs £9.1k. That totals £83.7k. Subtract £29.1k of price growth and the net cost is about **£54.6k**.
- **Renting:** rent £79.6k, minus £12.7k of interest earned, is a net cost of about **£66.9k**.

Each 1% a year of price growth moves the 5-year result by roughly £14k. Your decision is therefore mostly a bet on whether Leeds flat prices beat about 1% a year. Flats have historically grown more slowly than houses, and they can be slower to sell.

## Risks the spreadsheet doesn't show

1. **You'd have no cash buffer.** After completion you'd hold £0 and face a higher monthly outlay (£1,720 against £1,250). One boiler, job gap or special levy would go straight onto debt. This is the main reason to be cautious.
2. **Leasehold costs.** 115 years left on the lease is fine. However, service charges on flats often rise faster than inflation, and major-works bills (Section 20 notices) can run into thousands. Before you commit, ask for:
   - 3–5 years of service charge history
   - the reserve fund balance
   - any planned works
   - the cladding/fire safety (EWS1) position
   - whether the ground rent escalates
3. **The rate after the fix.** You assumed 4.6% from year 6. A remortgage at 6% would add about £100 a month, which only matters if you stay 7 years.
4. **The 5-year horizon is close to the minimum.** Buying and selling costs about £9k in total. If you sold at year 3, buying would very likely lose.

## Recommendation

**Buying is the reasonable choice if all three of these are true:**
- you're confident of staying 5 years or more
- the leasehold checks come back clean
- you can rebuild a buffer quickly

**I wouldn't buy with literally £0 left.** Some practical options:

- **Negotiate the price.** Every £5k off adds to your buffer and improves the maths. Asking prices on Leeds flats are often negotiable.
- **Wait 6–12 months** and save a £5–10k emergency fund. Rent rises cost you little over that period.
- **Consider a Lifetime ISA if you're under 40.** It adds a 25% government bonus (up to £1,000 a year) to first-home savings on properties up to £450k. The account must be open 12 months before you use it, so this only helps if you're willing to wait.

**Rent instead if** there's a real chance you'll move within about 4 years, or you think flat prices in your area will be flat or falling. In that case renting is safer and probably cheaper.

*This is general analysis based on your figures, not regulated advice. A mortgage broker can confirm affordability and product options, and a conveyancer should review the lease.*
