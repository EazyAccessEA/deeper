## Prompt

Should I rent or buy this flat?

=== MY SITUATION ===
- The flat: 2-bed in Leeds, asking price £280,000. Leasehold, 115 years left.
- Service charge plus ground rent: £1,800 a year.
- I have £46,500 saved. A 15% deposit is £42,000; legal fees, survey and other buying costs are about £4,500. I'm a first-time buyer.
- Mortgage offer: 85% loan, 4.6% fixed for 5 years, 25-year repayment term. Assume the rate stays 4.6% after the fix.
- Renting the same kind of flat nearby costs £1,250 a month. Rents here have gone up about 3% a year.
- If I rent, my savings sit in an account paying 4% a year, and I'd save any monthly difference there too.
- Upkeep on a flat like this is usually about 1% of the price a year.
- Selling costs (agent and legal) are about 1.5% of the sale price.
- I expect to stay about 5 years, maybe 7.
- I don't know what prices will do. The last few years round here were roughly +2% a year.

Nobody can answer questions before you reply. Do the task now with what's provided, and state any assumptions you make.

## Answer

# Rent or buy: the £280k Leeds flat

**Short answer: buying comes out ahead if prices keep rising at about 2% a year and you stay at least 5 years.** The margin is modest: about £12k after 5 years and £23k after 7. If prices rise less than about 1.2% a year, renting wins. The biggest practical problem is that buying uses every penny of your savings.

## Assumptions

- **Mortgage:** £238,000 at 4.6% over 25 years is **about £1,336 a month**. The rate stays at 4.6% throughout.
- **Stamp duty:** none. First-time buyers in England pay 0% up to £300k. Your £4,500 covers all other buying costs.
- **Service charge and ground rent:** £1,800, rising 3% a year.
- **Upkeep:** 1% of the flat's current value each year.
- **Rent:** £1,250 a month, rising 3% a year.
- **Renter's savings:** the renter keeps the £46,500 and saves the monthly difference at 4%. I've treated the interest as tax-free, as if held in an ISA.
- **Sale:** you sell at the end and pay 1.5% selling costs.
- **Comparison:** your net wealth in each case at year 5 and year 7.

## Yearly outgoings

| Year | Buy (mortgage + service + upkeep) | Rent | Buying costs more by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | £20,640 | £15,000 | £5,640 |
| 3 | £20,860 | £15,910 | £4,950 |
| 5 | £21,090 | £16,880 | £4,210 |
| 7 | £21,340 | £17,910 | £3,430 |

Owning costs about **£1,720 a month** at first, against £1,250 for renting. The gap shrinks as rent rises.

## Your wealth at the end (prices +2% a year)

| | 5 years | 7 years |
|---|---|---|
| Flat value | £309,100 | £321,600 |
| Minus selling costs and mortgage left | −£4,600 − £209,500 | −£4,800 − £196,100 |
| **Buyer's net equity** | **£95,100** | **£120,700** |
| **Renter's savings pot** | **£83,500** | **£97,700** |
| **Buying ahead by** | **≈ £11,600** | **≈ £23,100** |

## It all depends on house prices (5-year view)

| Price change per year | Buy vs rent after 5 years |
|---|---|
| −2% | Rent wins by ~£43,600 |
| 0% | Rent wins by ~£17,100 |
| **+1.2%** | **Break-even** |
| +2% | Buy wins by ~£11,600 |
| +4% | Buy wins by ~£42,600 |

Over 7 years the break-even drops to **about 0.9% a year**, because the buying and selling costs are spread over more years.

**Why the margin is thin:** in year 1, owning's costs that you never get back come to about £17,300. That's roughly £10,800 mortgage interest, £1,800 service charge, £2,800 upkeep and £1,900 of lost interest on your deposit. That's about £2,300 a year more than rent, before counting about £9,000 to buy and sell. Price growth has to make up that gap, and at 2% a year it does, but not by much.

## Things that could change the answer

1. **No emergency fund.** £42,000 deposit plus £4,500 costs uses all of your £46,500. A boiler, a lost job or a special levy on the building would have to go on debt. Keeping £5–10k back is wise. You could look at a 10% deposit (higher rate) or slightly cheaper flats.
2. **Remortgage rate in year 5.** If rates are higher when the fix ends, the 7-year case gets worse for buying.
3. **Leasehold risks.** 115 years is comfortably above the ~80-year point where lenders get nervous. But service charges can jump, and flats in Leeds city-centre blocks have resold weakly at times. Before committing, check:
   - the service charge history and the sinking fund
   - any cladding or EWS1 issues
   - the ground rent review terms
4. **Tax on savings.** If the renter's savings weren't in an ISA, tax on the interest would cut the renter's pot by about £2k. That tilts things slightly further towards buying.
5. **Moving early.** If you had to sell within 2–3 years, renting almost certainly wins, because the £9k buying and selling costs barely get spread out.

## Verdict

- **Lean towards buying** if you're fairly confident you'll stay 5–7 years, can afford £1,720 a month comfortably, and the leasehold paperwork checks out.
- **Rent for now** if you might move sooner, can't rebuild a cash cushion quickly, or think local flat prices will grow under about 1% a year.

The financial edge to buying is real but small. Stability and control over your home are probably as important as the numbers here.
