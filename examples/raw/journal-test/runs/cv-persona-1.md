## The top 5 fixes, in priority order

### 1. State your paid social budget (most important)
The advert sets a specific threshold of **£20k+ a month**, and many recruiters screen on exactly that. If you don't give a number, they'll assume it's small.
- **If your budget was £20k+/month:** say so in the first bullet of your current role.
- **If it was less:** still give the real figure, and show that you can handle scale. You could mention growth, results (ROAS, CPA) or how you split budget across channels. For example: *"Planned and managed £[X]k/month paid social budget across Meta and TikTok, growing it [X]% year on year while cutting cost per acquisition from £[X] to £[X]."* Do not inflate the figure, because budgets come up in interviews.

### 2. Put "GA4" on the page, in your current role
GA4 replaced Universal Analytics in July 2023, so your Lumen experience probably isn't GA4. If you use GA4 at Fernbrook, add a bullet such as *"Built monthly GA4 reports on campaign performance (traffic, conversion, revenue by channel) for the owners/leadership, which led to [decision, e.g. moving £X from Meta to TikTok]."* In Skills, change "Google Analytics" to **"GA4"**. ATS keyword filters may look for that exact term.

### 3. Explain the gap from March 2022 to February 2023
An 11-month gap with no explanation raises questions. A single line is enough, for example: *"Career break: travel / caring responsibilities / freelance marketing for [clients]."* Any freelance work done in that period also counts as relevant experience.

### 4. Replace vague bullets with what you did and what happened
Three bullets add nothing at the moment:
- **"Responsible for social media"** → What channels? How many followers? What growth or engagement? Who did you report to?
- **"Helped with campaigns"** → Name a campaign, your part in it, and the result.
- **"Supported the launch of the spring 2021 range, which sold out in 6 weeks"** → Good result, but "supported" hides your contribution. What did you actually do (emails, social, product page copy)?

### 5. Rewrite the profile
"Creative, hard-working, passion, team player, excellent communication" could describe anyone, and recruiters skip phrases like these. Your profile is the first thing they read, so it should match the advert directly.

## Smaller issues

- **Typo:** "Recieved" should be **"Received"**. A typo on a marketing CV is costly, because writing well is part of the job.
- **"Bean of the Year":** It's charming, but means nothing without context. Add why you won it, e.g. *"…for [the campaign/result]"*. Otherwise, cut it.
- **Agencies:** If you've ever briefed a printer, PR agency, photographer or ad agency, add it. Requirement 4 says "designers *and agencies*."
- **Line management:** Mention any current supervision, such as freelancers, cafe staff on social content, or interns. Show the intern as management: *"Line-managed a summer intern: set objectives, reviewed work, [outcome]."*
- **Retail angle:** Fernbrook sells online *and* in physical locations, which is a small version of Hartwell's model. Say so, e.g. *"…campaigns driving both online sales and footfall to 3 cafés."*
- **Outdoor interest:** If you hike, climb, camp or run, a one-line Interests section is worth adding for an outdoor brand. Keep it genuine.
- **Skills section:** Group the tools by job requirement: *Paid social: Meta Ads Manager, TikTok Ads Manager · Analytics: GA4, Excel · Email: Klaviyo · Design: Canva*. Add Looker Studio, Shopify or similar if you use them.
- **Dates:** Use one consistent format, e.g. "Feb 2023 – Present".

## Final note
If your real paid social budget is well under £20k/month, still apply. Give the honest figure, show strong results, and use your cover letter to explain how you'd scale up.
