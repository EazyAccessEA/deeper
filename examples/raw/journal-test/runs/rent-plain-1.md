## Prompt

Should I rent or buy this flat?

=== MY SITUATION ===
- The flat: 2-bed in Leeds, asking price £280,000. Leasehold, 115 years left.
- Service charge plus ground rent: £1,800 a year.
- I have £46,500 saved. A 15% deposit is £42,000; legal fees, survey and other buying costs are about £4,500. I'm a first-time buyer.
- Mortgage offer: 85% loan, 4.6% fixed for 5 years, 25-year repayment term. Assume the rate stays 4.6% after the fix.
- Renting the same kind of flat nearby costs £1,250 a month. Rents here have gone up about 3% a year.
- If I rent, my savings sit in an account paying 4% a year, and I'd save any monthly difference there too.
- Upkeep on a flat like this is usually about 1% of the price a year.
- Selling costs (agent and legal) are about 1.5% of the sale price.
- I expect to stay about 5 years, maybe 7.
- I don't know what prices will do. The last few years round here were roughly +2% a year.

Nobody can answer questions before you reply. Do the task now with what's provided, and state any assumptions you make.

## Answer

# Rent or Buy: Close Call at 5 Years, Leans Buy at 7

Buying comes out ahead only if prices rise more than about **1.2% a year** if you stay 5 years, or about **0.8% a year** if you stay 7. At the recent +2% a year, buying wins by about £12k over 5 years and £25k over 7. If prices stay flat, renting wins by about £16k. The biggest practical problem is that buying takes **all** of your savings and leaves no emergency fund.

## Assumptions
- **Stamp duty is £0.** First-time buyer relief covers prices up to £300k. I've assumed the £4,500 covers all other buying costs.
- **Owner costs stay fixed:** service charge and ground rent at £1,800 a year, and upkeep at 1% of the purchase price (£2,800 a year). In reality these usually rise, which slightly favours renting.
- **Savings interest is tax-free,** for example in a cash ISA. Outside an ISA, interest above your £1,000 personal savings allowance is taxed, which favours buying.
- **Same total spending either way.** As a renter, you save the gap between owning costs and rent each month.
- **No remortgage fees** after the 5-year fix, and all £4,500 of buying costs are lost for good.

## What the numbers mean
1. **The decision is a bet on price growth.** Your mortgage rate (4.6%) is higher than your savings rate (4%), and owning costs about £5,600 a year more at the start. Price growth has to make up that difference.
2. **Staying longer helps buying.** The one-off buying and selling costs (about £9k) are spread over more years, more of the loan gets repaid, and rent keeps rising. If 7 years is realistic, buying looks clearly better unless prices are flat or falling.
3. **Leeds flats can be slow to grow.** Two-bed leasehold flats, especially near the city centre, have often grown more slowly than houses. Check what has happened to flats in this building or development specifically. The area-wide +2% figure may not apply.

## Risks the numbers don't show
- **No cash buffer.** You'd spend all £46,500 on the purchase. One boiler, a special levy for building work, or a gap in your income could leave you stuck. I'd want £5–10k left over before buying.
- **Leasehold costs.** 115 years is a healthy lease length, but service charges can jump, and major works can cost thousands. Before you commit, ask for the last 3 years of service charge accounts, the reserve fund balance, any planned works, and the ground rent terms.
- **Refinancing in year 5.** If rates are higher when your fix ends, your costs rise. If you sell around year 5, you avoid most of that risk.

## Recommendation
- **Lean towards buying if** you're fairly confident you'll stay 7 years, the leasehold paperwork is clean, and you can keep a cash buffer.
- To keep a buffer, you could negotiate the price down. Getting it to about £270k saves around £10k across the deposit and loan, and reduces how much growth you need.
- **If 5 years is the more likely outcome,** it's roughly a coin flip, and renting keeps your options open at low risk. In that case, keep your savings in an ISA and invest the monthly difference rather than spending it.
