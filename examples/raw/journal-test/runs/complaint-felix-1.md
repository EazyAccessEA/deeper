## Call 1 reply

## 1. What a top professional would do that you aren't asking for

A senior complaints handler wouldn't treat this as "write a reply." They'd **close the case**, and the email would just report what they've already done.

- **Act before writing.** Approve the refund, book the collection and get the reference, then write. The email describes completed actions, not intentions.
- **Count the failures precisely.** Dana is angry about four things, not one:
  1. The late delivery.
  2. The damaged desk.
  3. The two broken callback promises.
  4. Something she may not know yet: we knew on 23 Oct that the parcel was stuck at the depot and never told her.

  A pro owns all four, by date, without arguing about whether it was "nine days."
- **Honour the choice she already made.** She asked for a full refund. Policy says that's her choice. So refund £198.95, including delivery, without making her ask twice or steering her to a replacement.
- **Calibrate compensation on purpose.** £25 is within the agent's authority. Two broken promises and a wasted week may justify more. The pro decides an amount, gives £25 now, and asks a manager for anything above that with a deadline. The email never promises money that hasn't been approved.
- **Defuse the chargeback without threatening.** If the refund is already issued, a chargeback gains her nothing. If she's already filed one, it could cause a double refund. Say this plainly and calmly.
- **Become the single named owner.** Policy forbids callback promises without a name and time, and that is exactly how she was failed twice. Priya gives her direct contact and a specific next touchpoint.
- **Fix things internally.** Raise a damage and delay claim against QuickRoute, flag the two missed callbacks for QA, and note the account.
- **Check the legal floor.** Under the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015, she is within the 30-day short-term right to reject. That entitles her to a full refund including standard delivery within 14 days. The policy's 5 working days beats the 14-day limit, so the pro can say so confidently.

- **Dana's photos,** to confirm the damage is transit damage and not something else.
- **Carrier data from QuickRoute.** That means the full tracking event log and the proof of delivery, including whether anything was signed "damaged."
- **The order confirmation she actually received,** for the exact delivery wording ("3–5 days" versus "3–5 working days").

- **The payment record.** Was it credit or debit? Has a chargeback or dispute already been opened?
- **Her full contact history and past orders.**

## 3. Methods and tools

- **A service-recovery structure.** Acknowledge each failure specifically, apologise without hedging, state what's done, compensate, and commit to a dated follow-through. No "any inconvenience" or "we value your feedback."
- **Timeline reconstruction from the records.** Every date in the email must trace to a log entry.
- **The real systems.**
  - CRM: assign Priya as case owner.
  - Payment processor: issue the refund and check dispute status.
  - Carrier portal: book the collection and lodge the claim.
- **A compensation rationale and a manager approval request,** if going above £25.
- **Plain-English editing.** Short paragraphs, the outcome in the first two lines, dates instead of "soon," and nothing defensive about the carrier.
- **A QA scorecard against the policy.** Did the reply include a named and timed contact, the refund including delivery, free collection, and the 5-working-day refund window?
