# Going deeper on your CV review

## 1. What a top professional would do that you're not asking for

A senior marketing recruiter or hiring manager wouldn't give general "feedback." They would **audit the CV against each essential criterion and ask whether the evidence is there.** For your CV, that check finds problems that comments on tone and layout would miss:

- **Essential #2 (£20k+/month paid social) has no evidence.** "Ran paid social campaigns on Meta and TikTok" gives no budget, results or scale. This is the requirement most likely to get you screened out, and the CV doesn't address it.
- **Essential #3 says GA4; your CV says "Google Analytics."** Your Lumen reporting (to March 2022) was almost certainly Universal Analytics, which GA4 replaced in July 2023. A sharp reader will notice. You need GA4 evidence from Fernbrook, or the CV implies you don't have it.
- **Your strongest metric is weak.** Since Apple Mail Privacy Protection (2021), open rates are inflated and unreliable. Experienced email marketers discount them. Click rate, revenue per recipient or attributed revenue from Klaviyo would carry far more weight.
- **There's an 11-month gap (Mar 2022 to Feb 2023)** with no explanation. Screeners notice gaps; a single line usually settles it.
- **"Responsible for," "Helped with," "Supported"** describe duties, not results. Lumen reads as junior for a *Senior* role.
- **Desirables are undersold.** Fernbrook is online plus three physical sites, which is multichannel retail. Hartwell is online plus 14 shops. Managing an intern counts toward "a team of 1–2."
- **Basics:** the "Recieved" typo; "Bean of the Year" means nothing without context (why did you win it?); the Profile is cliché and could be about anyone. It should be the 3-line answer to "why this job."
- **Reverse-engineering the employer:** looking at what Hartwell actually runs before writing a word.

## 2. The rawest material they'd start from

Not the CV, which is already a summary. They'd go to the sources:

- **Meta Ads Manager and TikTok Ads exports:** monthly spend, CPA, ROAS, and the campaigns you planned yourself versus ones you executed.
- **GA4 property:** reports, explorations or dashboards you built, and decisions they led to.
- **Klaviyo campaign and flow reports:** click rate, revenue per recipient, attributed revenue, any flows you built (welcome, abandoned basket).
- **The briefs themselves,** plus what came back and how it performed.
- **Performance reviews and the award citation.**
- **What you did Mar 2022 to Feb 2023.**
- **On Hartwell's side:** their live ads in the Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center, their emails (subscribe), their site, their accounts on Companies House, and the full job description if one exists beyond the advert.

## 3. Methods and tools

- **Requirement–evidence matrix:** each essential and desirable becomes a row, with the best CV evidence and a rating of strong, weak or missing.
- **XYZ / CAR bullet formula:** "Achieved X, measured by Y, by doing Z."
- **ATS keyword check:** the advert's exact terms (GA4, Klaviyo, briefs, £ budget, B2C) compared with the CV's, using a parser or a tool like Jobscan.
- **6–8 second skim test:** what a recruiter sees in the top third of page one.
- **Metric credibility check:** is each number one a specialist would trust?
- **Employer research:** using the Ad Library and email teardown to mirror Hartwell's channels and language.

## 4. What, other than you or me, would show it's right

- **An ATS parser's output:** does it extract your roles, dates and skills cleanly, and match the key terms?
- **A blind skim by a marketing hiring manager or senior marketer:** give them 10 seconds, then ask which of the five essentials they saw evidence for. The target is all five.
- **Your platform exports:** every number on the CV should match Ads Manager, GA4 or Klaviyo, so it holds up in interview or with a referee.
- **The outcome:** an interview invitation, and over several applications, your callback rate.

## Part 4: How to check it

**ATS keyword scan.** The parsed CV should contain these exact strings:

- **Channels and tools:** paid social, Meta, TikTok, GA4, Klaviyo, email campaigns
- **Advert language:** budget, £ figure, briefs, designers, agencies (only if true), B2C, retail, multichannel

Then run the CV through a parser such as Jobscan or Resume Worded, or paste it into an ATS-style form. Confirm that roles, dates and skills parse cleanly and the gap line reads as an entry, not as stray text.

**10-second skim test.** Show page one to a marketer for 10 seconds, then ask what they took away. They should be able to say:

1. 6+ years of B2C marketing.
2. Manages £[X]/month paid social on Meta and TikTok.
3. Uses GA4 now.
4. Klaviyo with a revenue result.
5. Briefs designers.

Most of that should come from the Profile and the first two Fernbrook bullets. Any essential they can't name is a gap.

**Tailoring the Profile from Hartwell's channels:**

- **Meta Ad Library** (search "Hartwell Outdoor"): number of live ads, formats (video, carousel, catalogue), seasonal themes, and messaging such as technical performance, sustainability or price.
- **TikTok Creative Center:** whether they're active on TikTok at all. If they aren't, your TikTok experience is something you can bring to them.
- **Their emails** (subscribe now): how often they send, whether they push product drops or editorial content, any visible welcome or abandoned-basket flows, and how they link online to shops ("click and collect", store events).
- **Website and Companies House:** company size and growth, to pitch at the right level.
- **Use what you find** to adjust one Profile line, e.g. "experience driving online-to-store traffic for a multichannel retailer." Mention any outdoor interest only if it's genuine.

**Claims you must be ready to defend in interview:**

- **The paid social budget figure:** how you split it, how you'd scale to £20k+, and your bidding and testing approach.
- **The GA4 decision story:** what you saw, what you changed, and what happened.
- **The Klaviyo revenue figure:** your attribution window and how you know the lift was yours.
- **Why open rates aren't your headline metric.** Raising MPP yourself shows expertise.
- **"Bean of the Year":** the specific story behind it.
- **The gap:** one confident sentence.
- **Managing an intern versus managing a team of 1–2:** how you'd manage, delegate and develop people.
- **Every number** should match your exports, since a referee or a live screen-share could test it.
