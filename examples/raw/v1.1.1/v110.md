## T1

```text
Write one Instagram caption for a jar of borage honey.

Before writing, ask me for: the photo it will go with, the label text (weight, price, where to buy), the harvest place and date, and anything the beekeeper said about this batch. Don't fill gaps with generic borage-honey descriptions; describe this jar.

How to make it:
- Open with one concrete fact only this jar has (provenance, harvest, colour or taste from an actual tasting), and make the first line work on its own in about 125 characters, before Instagram's "more" cut.
- Write in the voice of the brand's past captions if I give you some.
- Make no health or medicinal claims (no "soothing", "healing", "good for your throat"); stay within food-labelling rules.
- Pick a small set of niche hashtags (small-batch honey, local beekeeping, the region) rather than broad ones.
- End with one clear action (where to buy, or a question people can answer).

Check it before handing it over:
- Count the first line's characters and show the count.
- List every factual statement in the caption so the producer can confirm each one is true of this batch; any unconfirmed fact fails.
- List any wording that could read as a health claim; there must be none.
- After posting, compare saves and replies against the median of the account's last ten posts: above the median passes, below fails.

Deliver one caption.
```

## T2

```text
Evaluate the typesetting of my book manuscript and improve it. The inner margins look exceedingly large.

Before starting, ask me for: the source layout file (not just a PDF), the exported print PDF, and the printer's spec: trim size, binding method, paper stock and page count.

Evaluate:
- Judge margins on the open spread, not single pages. Check whether mirrored margins are on, whether the gutter is applied twice, and whether the binding allowance matches the binding method and page count. Treat "the inner margins are too large" as a claim to test: on a thick perfect-bound book a large inner margin can be correct.
- Measure, don't eyeball: margins in mm on each side, line length in characters (target 45 to 75), leading, baseline-grid consistency, widows and orphans, hyphenation runs, and font embedding.
- Run a PDF preflight (Acrobat Preflight, or pdfinfo and pdffonts) for page size, bleed, safe zones and fonts.

Improve:
- Reset the margins from trim size, binding and page count, with the inner margin chosen to allow for gutter loss, and make the text block read as centred on the open spread.
- Fix the measured problems in the source file, not in the PDF.

Check before handing back:
- Report the before and after measurements side by side.
- The preflight passes with no errors.
- A printed, bound proof opened at several typical spreads shows no text lost in the gutter and a visually centred text block; if possible, a typesetter or the printer's prepress team reviews it.
- Fail if any text runs into the gutter on the proof, if preflight reports an error, or if any measured figure is out of range without a stated reason.

Deliver: the evaluation, and the improved manuscript file.
```

## T3

```text
Help me sell my VW Polo and buy its replacement, perhaps a Golf.

Before starting, ask me for: the Polo's registration document, year, engine, trim, options, mileage, service history and any outstanding finance; my budget, how I'll pay (cash, finance, part-exchange), my annual mileage and typical use.

Selling the Polo:
- Value it for its exact spec from real transactions: valuation guides (e.g. Parkers, CAP HPI), instant-offer buyers (e.g. WeBuyAnyCar, Motorway), dealer part-exchange quotes, and completed private sales of comparable cars.
- Keep the sale separate from the purchase so value can't be shifted between the trade-in and the new car's price.
- Gather at least three written offers and choose the route (private, instant offer or part-exchange) on net money after time and risk.

Buying the replacement:
- Test "perhaps a Golf" against my budget and use: compare the Golf (right generation and engine) with its close rivals on total cost of ownership over my expected ownership period: price, finance APR, depreciation, insurance group, fuel or charging, servicing and tax.
- For any shortlisted car: a vehicle history check (outstanding finance, write-off, mileage), the official MOT history, open recalls, and an independent pre-purchase inspection.

Checks:
- Sale passes at or above the median of my written offers; fail below the best written offer I held.
- Purchase passes only with a clean history check, a passed independent inspection, and total cost within my stated budget; any one failing is a fail.

Deliver: the Polo sold and the new car bought.
```

## T4

```text
Can we move our Next.js app off Vercel to Cloudflare? If yes, do it.

Before starting, read the repo: package.json and the Next.js version, next.config, vercel.json, middleware, route handlers and their runtime exports, every use of revalidate/ISR, next/image, cron, and any Vercel SDK (KV, Postgres, Blob, Analytics). Ask me for the Vercel project's environment variables and recent traffic and function logs.

Answer "can we":
- Inventory every Vercel-specific feature the app depends on and, for each, name the Cloudflare equivalent (OpenNext Cloudflare adapter on Workers, R2, KV, D1, Durable Objects, Cron Triggers, Images) or say there is none.
- Check the Next.js version and every Node-only dependency against what @opennextjs/cloudflare supports.
- Treat "it's ready to move as is" as a claim to test; say plainly if the answer is "not yet" and what blocks it.

If yes, migrate:
- Build and deploy with @opennextjs/cloudflare and Wrangler to a preview on Workers, alongside the live Vercel deployment.
- Move environment variables and storage to their Cloudflare equivalents.
- Cut over DNS only after the checks below pass, with a rehearsed rollback to Vercel.

Checks:
- The existing test suite passes against the Cloudflare preview.
- A route-by-route diff of status codes, headers and key content between Vercel and Cloudflare shows no unexplained differences.
- ISR/revalidation updates pages within the same window as on Vercel.
- p95 latency and Core Web Vitals on the hot routes, measured with the same tool (WebPageTest or Lighthouse, plus a k6 load test), are at or better than on Vercel.
- A rollback to Vercel is rehearsed and completes within minutes.
- Fail on any broken route, any unexplained slower p95 on a hot route, or a rollback that doesn't work.

Deliver: the answer to "can we", and, if yes, the app running on Cloudflare.
```

## T5

```text
Tidy my Downloads folder.

Before changing anything, list every file with its size, type, date added and source (on macOS, the "where from" metadata via mdls), and compute a checksum for each.

How to tidy:
- Find duplicates by checksum, not by filename; a duplicate is only removable if an identical copy stays (in Downloads or elsewhere, such as Documents or my cloud drive).
- Sort what stays into a few folders by purpose (e.g. receipts and invoices, documents, installers, media), not by date.
- Delete nothing except checksum-confirmed duplicates. Move doubtful files (old installers, files with no other copy, anything that might be a receipt or contract) to a holding folder for me to review.

Check before handing back:
- Show before and after file counts and total size.
- Show that the count balances: files before = files kept + files in the holding folder + confirmed duplicates.
- Show the checksum match for every duplicate removed.
- Fail if any file's only copy is gone or the count doesn't balance.

Deliver: a tidy Downloads folder.
```
